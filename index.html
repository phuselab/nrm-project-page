The Neural Race Model

Stimulus-conditional stochastic accumulation, trained end-to-end on how people choose and how long they take.

Evidence for four alternatives accumulates until one hits the bound.

Each trial draws a new stimulus (the image beside the race). It sets the drift of every accumulator and the height of the bound: a clear image drives its own accumulator hard and the race ends early, a degraded one (blurred, noisy) leaves the evidence weak and ambiguous, so the race runs longer and errors become more likely.

Why this matters

A perceptual decision takes time, and that time is data. For fifty years, race and drift-diffusion models have explained choices and reaction times with a few interpretable parameters, but they are blind to the stimulus itself: whatever an image contains must first be squeezed into a single number such as "coherence".

Deep networks, on the other hand, see images but not time. Existing image-computable reaction-time models read out a proxy (confidence, entropy, the number of recurrent steps or FLOPs) that correlates with RT and must be rescaled to milliseconds afterwards.

The Neural Race Model keeps the race. It is a neural stochastic differential equation in which one accumulator per alternative races in continuous time towards a learnable, stimulus-dependent threshold. Drift, diffusion and threshold are neural networks conditioned on a stimulus embedding, and the whole model is trained end-to-end on the joint likelihood of human choices and reaction times.

How it works

The evidence state Xt ∈ ℝN holds one accumulator per alternative. A conditioning encoder turns the stimulus into a vector c, which parametrises every part of the race:

dXt = fθ(t, Xt, c) dt + gφ(t, Xt, c) dWt,    Θ(c) = exp hΘ(c)

  1. Condition on the stimulus

    A modular encoder maps images, videos, scalars or categories to c. For video, ct changes at every integration step.

  2. Race in continuous time

    Neural drift and diffusion push the accumulators; a neural threshold sets the bound, so urgency can depend on what is seen.

  3. Smooth the finish line

    A sigmoid of width 1/κ replaces the hard crossing. Monte Carlo trajectories give a soft first-passage density and choice probabilities, trained by NLL through the stochastic adjoint.

  4. Race for real at test time

    The surrogate is dropped. The first accumulator to cross Θ gives the choice; the crossing time gives the RT.

Psychometric function

Chronometric function

In a classical model, "stimulus strength" is one hand-picked number per trial. In the NRM, drift, noise and threshold are all computed from the stimulus embedding itself, so two images with the same nominal difficulty can still produce different races.

Making the finish line differentiable

A hard threshold crossing has no useful gradient. The NRM replaces it with a soft crossing probability σ(κ(X − Θ)), takes the positive increments of that probability along each simulated path, and averages over M trajectories to obtain a soft first-passage-time density. As κ grows, the soft density converges to the true one, at rate O(√Δt + 1/κ + 1/√M).

Drag κ to see the trade-off: small κ gives smooth gradients but a smeared, shifted density; large κ is exact but noisier to differentiate. The paper shows the budget-optimal sharpness scales as κ⋆ ∼ M1/3.

Results on three benchmarks

The NRM is compared against classical sequential-sampling models (LNR, LBA, DDM), image-computable RT models (RTify, cRNN) and anytime-prediction networks (CNet, ConvRNN, MSDNet, SCAN). Human curves are shown as a noise ceiling.

Videos of moving dots at seven coherence levels, 21 observers. The NRM is conditioned on a time-varying motion-energy signal and is the only model that reproduces both the rising psychometric curve and the falling chronometric curve.

Natural vs manmade scenes flashed for 7 ms, five discriminability levels. Images are encoded with a frozen DINOv2-small backbone. The NRM is the only model ranked first or second on accuracy and κ at every level, including the hardest near-boundary images where RTify and cRNN fall below chance.

Readable in the language of the brain

Because the NRM keeps the accumulate-to-bound structure, each component has a natural counterpart in the primate decision circuit. The paper offers this as interpretive commentary, not a mechanistic claim, and marks where the analogy breaks.
